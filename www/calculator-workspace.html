Formula learning

Cockcroft–Gault equation

What it measures

A practical estimate of renal clearance.

Cockcroft–Gault estimates creatinine clearance in adults using age, sex at birth, body weight, and serum creatinine. It has historically been used in renal drug-dosing references, but its limitations matter.

The formula

Adult equation · conventional units CrCl = [(140 − age) × weight × sex factor]
       ÷ (72 × SCr)
CrClEstimated creatinine clearance, in mL/min
AgePatient age, in years
WeightBody weight, in kilograms; follow your dosing protocol
Sex factor1 for the male equation; 0.85 for the female equation
SCrSerum creatinine, in mg/dL

Step-by-step worked example

  1. Start with the age termSubtract the patient’s age from 140: 140 − 68 = 72.
  2. Multiply by weightUse the protocol-appropriate weight: 72 × 72 kg = 5,184.
  3. Apply the sex factorFor this male-equation example, the factor is 1, so the numerator stays 5,184.
  4. Calculate the denominator72 × 1.4 mg/dL = 100.8.
  5. Divide and label the units5,184 ÷ 100.8 = 51.4 mL/min.

Clinical scenario

Medication review for an older adult

A pharmacist reviews a renally cleared medicine for a 68-year-old patient weighing 72 kg, with stable SCr of 1.4 mg/dL. The estimate prompts a check of the medicine’s monograph, the patient’s clinical trajectory, and local renal-dosing guidance—not an automatic dose change.

Common mistakes & safety notes

  • Mixing units. Convert SCr from µmol/L before using the conventional-unit equation.
  • Choosing weight automatically. Actual, ideal, or adjusted weight may differ by patient and protocol.
  • Using unstable renal function. A single changing SCr can make the estimate unreliable.

Do not use this educational example as a stand-alone dosing recommendation. Confirm the equation, units, weight strategy, and medicine-specific reference.
